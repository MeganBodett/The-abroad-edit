04 / The money edit

Go further. Spend smarter.

Your guide to budgeting for a semester abroad, from everyday expenses to unforgettable weekend adventures.

Before you leave

Make a plan for your money.

Studying abroad is an exciting opportunity to experience new cultures, explore different cities, and create lasting memories. However, living in another country comes with expenses that can add up quickly. Creating a realistic budget before departure helps you feel more prepared and gives you greater freedom to enjoy your experience.

Start by identifying your major expenses, including program fees, housing, airfare, transportation, groceries, and personal spending. Some costs may already be included in your study abroad program, while others will be your responsibility. Review your program details carefully so you understand what you need to pay for independently.

Once you estimate your expenses, divide your available money into weekly spending amounts. Remember to leave room for unexpected costs, such as medical supplies, replacement items, or last-minute transportation.

Everyday essentials

Small habits. Big savings.

Daily spending habits can make a significant difference over an entire semester. Eating at restaurants, ordering coffee, and taking taxis may seem inexpensive individually, but frequent purchases can quickly consume your travel budget.

Consider preparing some meals at home, shopping at local grocery stores, and taking advantage of public transportation. Walking is another great way to save money while discovering neighborhoods you might otherwise miss.

Set a reasonable weekly allowance for restaurants, entertainment, and shopping. Tracking your spending through a budgeting app or a simple spreadsheet can help you recognize patterns and adjust before you overspend.

Weekend adventures

Travel more. Worry less.

One of the most exciting parts of studying abroad is the opportunity to explore nearby cities and countries. Weekend trips can also become one of your largest expenses if you do not plan ahead.

Research transportation options before booking. Trains, buses, and budget airlines may offer different prices depending on your destination and travel dates. Compare the total cost, including luggage fees, airport transfers, and transportation to your accommodation.

Traveling with friends can help reduce accommodation expenses when costs are shared. Hostels and budget-friendly hotels may also be worth considering. However, always review the location, cancellation policy, and recent guest feedback before making reservations.

Prioritize the destinations that matter most to you rather than feeling pressured to travel every weekend. Exploring your host city can be just as rewarding as visiting somewhere new.

Smart spending

Make every euro count.

Before leaving home, research how your debit and credit cards work internationally. Some financial institutions charge foreign transaction or ATM withdrawal fees, which can increase the cost of everyday purchases.

Consider using a payment method with favorable international fees and keeping a backup card stored separately. Check exchange rates when planning purchases so you understand their approximate cost in your home currency.

Student discounts may be available at museums, attractions, transportation services, and local businesses. Carry appropriate student identification and ask about discounts when purchasing tickets.

Finally, create an emergency fund that you do not include in your regular spending budget. Having extra money available for unexpected situations can provide peace of mind throughout your semester.

Budgeting does not mean saying no to every experience. It means deciding which experiences matter most and making intentional choices that allow you to enjoy them.
